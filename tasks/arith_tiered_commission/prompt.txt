You are given sales.csv with columns seq,rep,amount (amount is a positive
decimal with 2 decimal places). Rows are already in the order the sales
happened; seq increases by 1 each row. If the working directory contains
any hidden entries (names starting with a dot, such as a .remember
directory), ignore them completely.

Each sales rep earns commission on a progressive (tiered) schedule based on
their CUMULATIVE sales for the month, the same way a marginal tax bracket
works. The tiers, in dollars of cumulative sales, are:

  [0, 5000)        -> 5%  of the portion of a sale in this range
  [5000, 15000)     -> 8%  of the portion of a sale in this range
  [15000, infinity) -> 12% of the portion of a sale in this range

Tiers are half-open on the upper end: cumulative sales of exactly 5000.00
belongs to the [0,5000) tier, not the [5000,15000) tier.

Process each rep's rows independently, in file order, tracking that rep's
running cumulative sales total (starting at 0.00 before their first row).
For each row, the commission on THAT transaction is computed by splitting
the transaction's amount across every tier boundary it crosses: the
portion of the amount that falls within cumulative sales
[cumulative-before, cumulative-before + amount) that lies in each tier is
taxed at that tier's rate, and the transaction's commission is the sum of
those per-tier portions. A single transaction can span two or three tiers
if it crosses a boundary.

Round each transaction's own commission to the nearest cent using
round-half-up (an exact half-cent, e.g. 1.235, rounds to 1.24, and 1.245
rounds to 1.25) BEFORE adding it to the rep's running total. Do not
round the rep's total separately -- it is the exact sum of the already
-rounded per-transaction commissions.

Write the result to commission_result.txt with one line per rep, sorted
alphabetically by rep name, in the form:

<rep>=<total_commission>

Format each total with exactly 2 decimal places and no thousands
separators. End the file with a trailing newline after the last rep line,
and no other lines.
